What Is Online Tracking?

Online tracking is when websites and other online services collect information about a person's activity on the internet. This information can include which websites someone visits, what they click on, and what products they search for.

Companies may use this information to understand their visitors, improve their websites, and display personalized advertisements.

Common Online Tracking Methods

1. Tracking Cookies

Some cookies are used to recognize users across multiple websites. Advertising companies may use these cookies to learn about browsing interests and display targeted advertisements.

2. Browser Fingerprinting

Browser fingerprinting collects information about a user's browser and device. This can include screen size, browser settings, language, and other details. When combined, this information may help identify a browser without using cookies.

3. Tracking Pixels

Tracking pixels are small resources placed on websites or inside emails. When loaded, they can send information to a server, such as whether a page or email was opened.

What Is Targeted Advertising?

Targeted advertising is when companies display advertisements based on information about a user's interests or behavior.

For example, someone who searches for running shoes might later see advertisements related to athletic clothing or shoes.

Although personalized advertisements can be useful, some people are uncomfortable with how much information companies may collect about their online activities.

Privacy Concerns

Online tracking raises questions about how personal information is collected, stored, and shared.

  • Users may not know they are being tracked.
  • Browsing habits may be collected over time.
  • Information may be shared with third parties.
  • Tracking can make users feel less private online.

How Can Users Reduce Online Tracking?

There are several steps people can take to improve their online privacy.

  1. Review browser privacy settings.
  2. Block third-party cookies when possible.
  3. Delete cookies and browsing data regularly.
  4. Review cookie consent options on websites.
  5. Use browsers with tracking protection features.
  6. Avoid sharing unnecessary personal information.

These steps may reduce tracking, but they cannot guarantee complete anonymity on the internet.

Privacy Tip: Review your browser's privacy settings and remove cookies you no longer need.
