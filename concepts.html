Understanding Online Privacy

Learning about online privacy starts with understanding some important terms. These six concepts explain how websites remember information, track activity, and give users control over their data.

1. HTTP Cookies

HTTP cookies are small pieces of data that a website sends to a web browser. The browser can store these cookies and send them back to the website during future requests.

Example: A shopping website uses a cookie to remember your shopping session.

2. First-Party Cookies

First-party cookies are associated with the website a person is visiting. They are often used to remember login sessions or website preferences.

Example: A website remembers your preferred language when you return.

3. Third-Party Cookies

Third-party cookies are associated with a domain different from the website a person is visiting. They have been used for advertising and tracking across websites.

Example: An advertising service may use third-party cookies to recognize a browser on different websites.

4. Browser Fingerprinting

Browser fingerprinting is a method of collecting details about a browser and device to help recognize it. These details may include screen resolution, language, and browser settings.

Example: A tracking service combines browser information to recognize a device without relying on cookies.

5. Tracking Pixels

Tracking pixels are small resources used to collect information when a webpage or email loads. They can help organizations measure page visits or email openings.

Example: A marketing email contains an image that reports when the email is opened, if the image is loaded.

6. Cookie Consent

Cookie consent is a way for websites to ask users for permission to store or access certain types of cookies. Depending on privacy laws, some cookies may require permission before they are used.

Example: A website displays a message asking whether you want to accept or reject optional tracking cookies.

Why These Concepts Matter

Understanding these terms can help people make better decisions about their online privacy. Cookies can improve the browsing experience, but tracking technologies can also collect information about users.

Learning how these technologies work helps users understand privacy settings and make informed choices.
