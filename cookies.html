What Is a Cookie?

A cookie is a small piece of information that a website can save in your web browser. Cookies help websites remember certain information about visitors.

For example, when you log into a website and stay logged in while moving between pages, cookies may help the website recognize your session.

Cookies can make browsing more convenient, but some cookies can also be used to track activity across different websites.

How Do Cookies Work?

When you visit a website, its server may send a cookie to your browser. Your browser can store the cookie and send it back when making future requests.

  1. You visit a website.
  2. The website sends a cookie to your browser.
  3. Your browser stores the cookie.
  4. Your browser sends the cookie back on applicable future requests.
  5. The website uses the cookie to recognize your session or preferences.

Different Types of Cookies

Cookies can be classified by where they come from and how long they last.

Cookie Type Purpose Example
First-Party Created by the website you visit. Remembering website preferences.
Third-Party Associated with another website or service. Advertising or cross-site tracking.
Session Usually removed when the browser session ends. Temporary session information.
Persistent Remains until its expiration or until it is deleted. Remembering language preferences.

Benefits of Cookies

  • Help users stay logged into websites.
  • Remember language and display preferences.
  • Support online shopping carts.
  • Improve the browsing experience.

Privacy Concerns

Although cookies can be useful, some are used for tracking and advertising. This can raise privacy concerns when information about browsing activity is collected across websites.

Users can manage cookies through their browser settings and can delete stored cookies when needed.
